* Sunrise and sunset
%%(diary-sunrise-sunset)
Set calendar-latitude and calendar-longitude in the init file (for example =(setq calendar-latitude 35.994 calendar-longitude -78.8986)=), and every day of the agenda shows its sunrise and sunset.

* Sun times today only, in your own format
%%(when (equal date (calendar-current-date)) (let ((l (solar-sunrise-sunset date))) (format "Sun times: 🌅 %s | 🌇 %s | ⌛ %s" (apply 'solar-time-string (car l)) (apply 'solar-time-string (cadr l)) (nth 2 l))))
The test (equal date (calendar-current-date)) is true only on the current day. To show the length as 9h56 instead of 9:56, wrap it: (string-replace ":" "h" (nth 2 l)). A time such as 07:05 anywhere in a diary line is taken as the entry's time, so the form '(24-hours "h" minutes) keeps the sunrise in the text. Add =(setq calendar-time-display-form '(24-hours ":" minutes))= to the init file for 24-hour times without the zone.

* Sexp timestamps in an Org file
** Pay rent <%%(diary-date t 1 t)>
Shows on the 1st of every month, like a repeating timestamp.
** Team meeting <%%(diary-float t 4 4) 10:00-11:00>
Shows on the fourth Thursday of each month at 10:00.
** Report
SCHEDULED: <%%(diary-float t 1 1)>
Shows as Scheduled: on the first Monday of each month.
** Dentist <2026-12-03 Thu 09:30>
A timestamp in the heading puts the heading on that day.
For computed text such as the sun times above, put the sexp on its own line at the left margin; a <%%(...)> stamp always shows its heading as written.
